# Objectives & Backward Design

Focus: define clear, measurable outcomes first and design assessments that provide evidence of those outcomes.

Core steps:
- Identify desired performance (what learners should be able to DO).
- Define acceptable evidence (assessment criteria, rubrics).
- Design learning experiences to scaffold toward the evidence.

## Bloom's Taxonomy and Revised Bloom's Taxonomy

For cognitive objectives, explicitly state the taxonomy version and level. Use the taxonomy required by the brief; otherwise default to Bloom's Taxonomy (1956).

| Version | Cognitive levels in order |
|---------|---------------------------|
| Bloom's Taxonomy (1956) | Knowledge, Comprehension, Application, Analysis, Synthesis, Evaluation |
| Revised Bloom's Taxonomy (Anderson and Krathwohl, 2001) | Remember, Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create |

The revision is not simply a change from nouns to verbs: Create replaces Synthesis and follows Evaluate, and the framework distinguishes cognitive processes from knowledge types.

For the revised taxonomy, identify both the cognitive process and the knowledge dimension: factual, conceptual, procedural, or metacognitive.

With either taxonomy, a well-written objective must state what learners will observably do, under what conditions, and to what standard. Observable learner actions are not exclusive to the revised taxonomy; taxonomy category labels alone are not learning objectives.

Choose the level based on the thinking the task actually requires, not its action verb alone. Write observable evidence even for objectives at Understand or Comprehension. Align assessment demands with the intended level, and do not require every lesson to reach the highest level.

## Affective and Psychomotor Objectives

Bloom's cognitive taxonomy and its revision do not classify all learning outcomes. Identify the learning domain, meaning the kind of learning intended, not the subject being taught. Use the taxonomy required by the brief; otherwise use these defaults:

| Learning domain | Default taxonomy | Levels in order |
|-----------------|------------------|-----------------|
| Affective: attitudes, values, and dispositions | Krathwohl, Bloom, and Masia (1964) | Receiving, Responding, Valuing, Organization, Characterization by a Value or Value Complex |
| Psychomotor: physical movement, coordination, and skilled action | Simpson (1972) | Perception, Set, Guided Response, Mechanism, Complex Overt Response, Adaptation, Origination |

State the taxonomy and level for each objective. Do not assign cognitive levels to affective or psychomotor objectives or require all three domains in every lesson.

- For affective objectives, assess observable participation, choices, or sustained behavior. Do not infer private beliefs or a stable disposition from a single response.
- For psychomotor objectives, specify relevant criteria such as accuracy, coordination, or safe execution. Do not classify a task as psychomotor merely because it involves using equipment.
- All domains require observable learner actions, conditions, and success criteria.

## Writing Objectives

Quick objective template (ABCD):
- Audience: who
- Behavior: observable action
- Condition: tools/constraints
- Degree: performance standard

Example: "Given two sources and a credibility checklist (Condition), a learner (Audience) will justify a source selection (Behavior) meeting all three rubric criteria: relevance, authority, and corroboration (Degree)."

Default taxonomy classification: Bloom's Taxonomy (1956), Evaluation.

If the brief specifies Revised Bloom's Taxonomy (2001), classify the same objective as Evaluate; conceptual knowledge of source-credibility criteria.

Quick alignment checklist:
- Objective present: Yes/No
- Learning domain, taxonomy, and level identified for each objective: Yes/No
- Knowledge dimension identified when using the revised taxonomy: Yes/No
- Evidence defined: Yes/No
- Assessment mapped: Yes/No
