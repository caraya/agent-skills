# Instructional Analysis

Purpose: run rapid, evidence-driven analysis to define who the learners are, what they need to do, and the constraints that affect design.

When to use: at project kickoff, before writing objectives or content.

Core activities:
- Learner profile: educational context or roles; prerequisite skills, knowledge, and behaviors, distinguishing documented evidence from assumptions; learner-reported goals and time constraints when available.
- Needs assessment: use gap analysis to identify and prioritize needs, investigate their causes, and determine whether instruction is an appropriate response.
- Task analysis: break complex skills into teachable sub-skills.
- Constraints inventory: distinguish externally imposed requirements and limits (institutional schedules, approved budgets, available facilities and platforms, accessibility and localization requirements) from internal capacity limits (design and preparation time, instructor expertise, assessment and feedback workload); identify what the designer or instructor can adjust and what requires external approval or support.
- Expert blind-spot review: help the designer or instructor identify potentially unstated steps and decisions; analyze expert think-alouds and recent-learner feedback when provided, and label unverified blind spots as hypotheses.
- Distinguish a learning gap from a process, resource, or environment problem.

Templates
- Learner profile fields: role or educational context, prior knowledge, relevant experience, resources, pain points, availability.
- Needs statement: "Current: X. Desired: Y. Gap: Z. Success metric: M."
